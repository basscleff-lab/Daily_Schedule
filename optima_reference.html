🪟 Casement Windows

Window
What It Is Hinged on the side; cranks outward like a door up to 90 degrees.
Best Used For Living rooms, bedrooms, and any spot where maximum airflow and the tightest winter seal are needed.
Key Highlights
  • Multi-point locking mechanism pulls sash flush against frame (tightest draft seal).
  • Cranks a full 90° for easy reach to clean outside glass from inside.
  • Folding low-profile crank handle won't catch on blinds or curtains.
💬 What to say to client

"Our casement windows crank open outward for maximum breeze, and their multi-point lock gives the tightest airtight seal against cold Ontario winters."

🪟 Awning Windows

Window
What It Is Hinged along the top; cranks open outward from the bottom, forming a small protective awning.
Best Used For Bathrooms, over the kitchen sink, or paired below large picture windows.
Key Highlights
  • Can stay open for fresh air during a light rain without water entering.
  • Effortless crank operation over kitchen counters.
  • High acoustic insulation and dual-side compression locks.
💬 What to say to client

"Awning windows hinge at the top and push out from the bottom—they're perfect for kitchens and bathrooms because you can keep them open for fresh air even when it's raining."

🪟 Single & Double Hung

Window
What It Is Traditional vertical sliding windows. Single: bottom sash moves up. Double: both top and bottom sashes move.
Best Used For Colonial / traditional homes, bedrooms, or walls next to walkways/patios where an outward crank would obstruct foot traffic.
Key Highlights
  • Sashes tilt inward 90° so you can clean the exterior glass safely from inside (no ladder needed!).
  • Double-hung allows hot air to escape from top while cool air enters from bottom.
  • Heavy-duty constant-force balance springs for featherlight opening.
💬 What to say to client

"Hung windows slide vertically, and both sashes tilt right into the room so you can easily wipe down the outside glass without ever stepping onto a ladder."

🪟 Slider Windows (Single / Double / End-Vent)

Window
What It Is Horizontal sliding windows. Single has 1 moving sash; Double has 2; End-Vent has a big center fixed window flanked by two sliding vents.
Best Used For Wide window openings, bedrooms, basements, or areas opening onto decks and porches.
Key Highlights
  • Smooth brass or tandem nylon roller glides for effortless one-finger operation.
  • Sashes tilt or lift out for easy cleaning.
  • Very cost-effective for wider openings compared to multiple casements.
💬 What to say to client

"Our slider windows glide smoothly left-to-right. They're ideal for wide wall spaces and areas where you don't want a window swinging outside into a deck or walkway."

🪟 Fixed / Picture Windows

Window
What It Is Stationary, non-opening window engineered for maximum glass area and unobstructed view.
Best Used For Living rooms, high stairwells, dining rooms, or framing a scenic backyard view.
Key Highlights
  • Highest energy rating of all windows (zero moving parts, joints, or draft points).
  • Brings in maximum natural daylight.
  • Frequently paired with operable casement or awning flankers on either side.
💬 What to say to client

"Picture windows are stationary for panoramic views and natural light. Because they don't open, they offer the absolute highest insulation rating and zero draft potential."

🪟 Bay & Bow Windows

Window
What It Is Windows projecting outward from the home wall. Bay: 3 distinct panels (usually picture center + 2 angled flankers). Bow: 4 to 6 panels in a gentle curve.
Best Used For Living rooms, master suites, adding interior seating or plant shelves, and dramatic front curb appeal.
Key Highlights
  • Captures sunlight from multiple directions throughout the day.
  • Creates extra interior floor space and an architectural focal point.
  • Flankers can be configured as casements for cross-ventilation.
💬 What to say to client

"Bay and bow windows project outward to bring in light from all angles and create an interior shelf or bench space. Bay has three panels, while Bow creates a gentle graceful curve."

⚙️ Double vs. Triple Pane Glass

Spec
The Difference Double Pane: 2 glass panes + 1 argon gas pocket (Standard).
Triple Pane: 3 glass panes + 2 argon gas pockets (+~$150–$400/window).
When To Recommend Triple Pane
  • Home is on a busy street or highway (exceptional soundproofing).
  • Severe north or west wind exposure / cold drafts.
  • Homeowner wants zero winter window condensation and maximum energy savings.
💬 What to say to client

"Double pane is already very high-performance, but if you're on a noisy street or want the absolute warmest window with zero winter condensation, triple pane is fantastic. Our specialist can price both options side-by-side during your visit."
